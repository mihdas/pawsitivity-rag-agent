## Why are there no breed puppies for adoption?
The reason you will very rarely find purebred for adoption is because no one abandons them when they are young and cute. People only abandon them when they realize that dogs cost money, resources and time to take care of. Slowly the craze of their cuteness wears off and reality hits. Whenever there are young purebred dogs up for adoption, the response is crazed because Indian 'dog lovers' tend to be breed obsessed. There are many mixed/indie puppies and abandoned adult purebred dogs available for adoption.

## Why should you consider adopting a rescue?
A rescue or a shelter dog/cat have already suffered in their lives, and are equally deserving and in need of homes. Breed dogs do not come along unless purposefully breeded for supplying demands of the market. If you are keen on a breed dog even then you should consider shelters which are full of mixed breed and purebred dogs abandoned after they were no longer required to fulfill the needs of their families. Left behind as an afterthought. If you are extremely keen on a puppy then we suggest you adopt an indie/mixed breed puppy. We can tell you more about indies and their characteristics if you are interested in at least giving them a chance or expanding your knowledge. The spirit of adoption is not to make too many demands or to get a free puppy of your choice. Of course not all dogs are fit for a home but a single picture or a breed does not determine that. Contrary to how most people decide to get home a companion animal, it should be a well researched and informed decision.

## Why are breed based demands often misplaced?
Instead of fixating on a breed, the aim should be to understand through the adoption coordinators what goes behind taking care of an animal. There is often certain constraints we might not even be aware off unless we understand the specific needs of the animal. Each animal is different despite their breed and breed based biases lead to a lot of animals being abandoned. When you are ill-prepared for such a big decision even the slightest set-back can be disheartening. If you still feel unsure about adoption, checkout our information section for fostering and sign up to be a foster before you commit to adopting.

## Why is your adoption or foster form so detailed?
The form is intentionally detailed because it helps people think through the realities of adoption or fostering before they commit, including time, finances, care, and long-term responsibility. It also helps us understand each lead better before the house check stage and identify whether the match is likely to be suitable. This is why we encourage everyone to be as thorough on their form as possible so we get a complete idea on how to guide you best.

## Why do you ask people to answer the form honestly and without using AI?
The goal of the form is not to test whether someone has perfect knowledge, but to understand how prepared they are and how best they can be supported. Honest answers are far more useful than polished ones because they help us assess readiness and guide adopters responsibly.

## What happens after I fill out the adoption or foster form?
After the form is reviewed, the next step is usually a house check and a more detailed conversation about your home, routine, finances, travel plans, experience, and expectations. This helps ensure that the animal is placed in a home that is safe, realistic, and prepared.

## What do you look for during a house check?
A house check looks at practical things such as house size, where the animal will stay, who the primary caregivers are, how long the animal will be alone, allergies, finances, and whether all family members are on board. It also covers travel plans, relocation possibilities, access to a vet, diet plans, sterilisation, vaccination, and training expectations where relevant.

## Can first-time adopters still adopt from you?
Yes, first-time adopters can absolutely be considered, but they need to be open to learning and prepared to take responsibility seriously. The process is designed not just to screen adopters, but also to support them with the right information and preparation.


## Do I need to have everything ready before adoption?
While you don't need to have everything perfect, it's important to be as prepared as possible. This includes having a safe space for your new pet, understanding the commitment involved, and being ready to provide proper care and attention.adopters are expected to arrange the basic essentials before the animal comes home, such as bowls, safe travel gear, bedding, litter supplies or walking equipment, and other case-specific items. In your process, people may also be asked to share a picture of the essentials purchased, which helps confirm preparedness and avoid mistakes like wrong sizes or unsuitable products.

## Why do you require a contract?
The adoption contract is there to protect the animal and clearly set out the adopter’s responsibilities around care, safety, veterinary treatment, follow-ups, and long-term commitment. It also makes clear that the animal cannot be abandoned, sold, given away, transferred, or otherwise disposed of without prior communication and approval.

## Is sterilisation mandatory?
If the animal is not already sterilised at the time of adoption, the adopter is expected to get this done at the appropriate age through a licensed veterinary doctor and not use the animal for breeding or mating. This is treated as a serious part of responsible adoption, not an optional extra.

## What is your position on vaccination, deworming, and preventive care?
Vaccination, deworming, and related preventive care are treated as essential parts of responsible guardianship in your process. During screening, adopters are specifically asked what they know about these areas and how much support they may need to follow through properly.

## Do you have different expectations for dogs and cats?
Yes, some expectations are species-specific, such as litter box readiness and cat-proofing for cats, and training, leash safety, and appropriate walking equipment for dogs. Adopter guidance is personalised for each animal, city, and case rather than treated as one-size-fits-all.

## Will I receive guidance after adoption?
Yes, your adoption materials include personalised post-adoption guidance, including practical care information, adjustment support, diet suggestions, health reminders, and trainer or vet references where needed. We are always available to guide you for the well-being of your companion pet no matter how long ago you adopted them through us.

## What happens if I travel often or plan to relocate?
Travel frequency, boarding options, support systems, and relocation plans are all discussed during the screening process because they directly affect long-term stability for the animal. Adopters are also expected to communicate any major change of address or relocation plans so the animal’s welfare is not compromised.

## Can I let my adopted cat roam outdoors?
Your contract language makes clear that adopted cats are expected to live as companion animals inside the home and should not be allowed to roam freely outside unsecured spaces. It also requires adopters to make the home safe by securing areas such as windows, balconies, doors, and shafts from which a cat could escape.

## What training methods do you recommend?
We support positive reinforcement methods and reject punishment-based tools such as prongs and shock collars. For dogs especially, adopters are expected to be open to training support before problems escalate.

## Will you stay in touch after adoption
Yes, your contract includes regular post-adoption updates so that the animal’s adjustment, health, and well-being can be monitored over time. This reflects our approach that adoption is an ongoing responsibility and relationship, not a one-time handover.

## How should I prepare my home before bringing a cat home?
Before adoption, your home should be made safe, quiet, and predictable for a cat to settle into comfortably. This includes arranging essentials in advance, deciding where the cat will stay, and securing all escape-prone areas such as windows, balconies, doors, and shafts.

## How do I prepare my home before I bring home a dog?
Before bringing home a dog, prepare one calm, safe area with a bed or crate, food and water bowls, and a few toys, and decide in advance which parts of the home the dog will be allowed to access. Dog-proof the house by securing cords, chemicals, garbage, doors, windows, balconies, and anything chewable or easy to swallow, and use gates if some spaces need to stay off-limits. It is also important to have the basics ready beforehand such as leash or harness, bedding, food plan, vet access, and a clear routine for walks, training, supervision, travel, costs, and how long the dog may be left alone.
